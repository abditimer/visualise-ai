Independent illustration · synthetic inputs

What shapes the cost?

Explore a simplified retirement-income calculation. Higher discount rates reduce the present value; a longer payment horizon increases it.

Illustrative present value

Toy calculation: sum annual income ÷ (1 + discount rate)^year over the selected horizon. Fixed annual payments, no mortality, fees or inflation. This is an actuarial illustration, not a trained ML model. Original company work belongs to Legal & General; this demo is independently authored.

Independent illustration · synthetic inputs

How far does the curve reach?

Move the synthetic survival horizon to see how the curve and expected years of payments change.

Expected payment-years within 40 years

Synthetic survival S(t) = exp(−ln(2) × (t / median)^2). Expected payment-years = sum of S(t) at years 1–40. The median marks 50% survival; it is not the mean. No real demographic data or company longevity model is used. Original work belongs to Legal & General.

A tiny additive tree illustration: a base estimate plus two simple decision rules. Change the synthetic inputs to see each contribution.

Our illustrative offer

Rules: base 100; contract size > 60 adds 12 (otherwise −4); complexity > 5 adds 8 (otherwise −3). Illustrative offer fixed at index 100. One workload. Many partitions.

Explore a conceptual healthcare feature pipeline: join policy and encounter tables → aggregate features → score a model. Workers process partitions in parallel, with an overhead for coordination.

Simulated speed-up
Single worker
Parallel plan

Simulation only; Spark does not run in this browser. Assumed single-worker time = rows × 4 seconds. Parallel time = serial time × (0.15 + 0.85 / workers) + 0.8 × (workers − 1) seconds. Balanced partitions and a fixed 15% serial component; actual performance depends on joins, shuffle, skew, storage and cluster configuration. Original work belongs to Capco and its client; this independent example contains no company code or health data.
